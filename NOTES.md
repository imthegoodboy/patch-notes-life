# Notes

The judge's brief, in order: a single-line patch note for a real-life or body bug that reads as comedy; genuinely funny, not just the word "bug" in a sentence; a real release-note register (version number, terse, the fix stated as shipped); a specific, recognisable bug; no padding within 160 characters.

Candidates considered:
1. "v1.4.2: Fixed issue where walking into a room garbage-collected the reason you walked in." Strong, very recognisable; the laugh is a little gentler.
2. "v29.0.0: Back pain now ships enabled by default. No action required. No action possible." Good, a bit bleak.
3. "v8.1.3: Sneeze no longer cancelled at 95% loading. Known issue: still triggered by sun." Two jokes competing.
4. "v2.0.1: Leg sleep mode patched; limb now wakes with full pins-and-needles." Mild.
5. "v30.0.0: Hangover recovery raised from 1 day to 3. Working as intended." Funny, narrower audience.
6. "v4.0.1: Perfect comeback now arrives in the shower, 6 hours after the argument, as designed." Close second.
7. "v2.6.0: Hiccup handler no longer recurses. Root cause unknown." Too dry.
8. "v3.2.1: Fixed bug where embarrassing memories from 2009 replayed at 2 AM. They now replay at 4 AM, in HD." **Chosen.**

Why #8: everyone knows the 2 AM cringe replay (criterion 4), it is written like a real changelog with a version and "Fixed bug where" (criterion 3), and the joke is that the "fix" makes it worse, then worse again with "in HD" as the last two words. 105 characters, under the 160 cap.

Revisions: the first draft ("Fixed a bug ... now replay at 3 AM.") was tightened and given the "in HD" sting; then a 1-160 character guard was added.
